The field notes · 01

A skincare routine you can return to.

A clear morning and evening order, a few products you know, and permission to keep it simple.

By ASMR Beauty Pro·October 3, 2026

In this guide

The short answer

Cleanse. Moisturize. Protect.

For a straightforward routine, start with a gentle cleanser and moisturizer. In the morning, finish with broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher before going outdoors. At night, cleanse and moisturize. If you use a treatment, follow its directions and place it after cleansing, before moisturizer. The American Academy of Dermatology recommends this simple foundation.

01 / Morning

Begin with what matters.

  1. Cleanse gently. Choose a cleanser that feels comfortable on your skin. Avoid scrubbing; a routine that irritates you is hard to keep.
  2. Apply a treatment, if you use one. Follow the label or your clinician’s directions. You do not need to add a serum just to fill a step.
  3. Moisturize, then protect. Choose a moisturizer that suits your skin. Before going outdoors, apply broad-spectrum, water-resistant SPF 30+ sunscreen to exposed skin; reapply every two hours while outdoors and after swimming or sweating.

Why this order? Read the dermatologist guidance on product order and sunscreen application.

02 / Evening

Let the day end simply.

  1. Cleanse. Wash away the day with a gentle cleanser, without abrasive scrubbing.
  2. Use a treatment only if it belongs in your routine. Follow its directions or the plan from your clinician.
  3. Moisturize. Keep the final step comfortable and repeatable.

There is no prize for more layers. Dermatologists note that using too many products can irritate skin. See the AAD’s guidance on order and product count.

“Enough” can be a routine you understand and can change when life changes.

03 / Keep it flexible

Your shelf, your pace.

Start with products you already own and tolerate. If you introduce something new, test it on a small area first and give yourself room to notice a reaction. The AAD’s product-testing instructions explain how. Stop using a product that causes a concerning reaction and ask a dermatologist for help if irritation persists.

Review your routine when seasons, preferences, or products change. Keep each step legible: what it is, when you use it, and whether it still works for your day. There is no need to add a product just because a trend says you should.
